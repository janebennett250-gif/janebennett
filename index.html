Drawer 01 The Problem

Most authors don't have a quality problem.
They have a visibility problem.

Your book might be beautifully written and perfectly packaged — but if readers can't find it, none of that matters. A discovery gap is the space between where your book is positioned and where your ideal readers are actually looking.

"

Reviews are great, but nobody's finding the book.
4+ stars, glowing feedback — yet BSR is stuck past #500,000. Quality and visibility are separate problems.

"

I launched and it just... disappeared.
A brief spike, then silence. The critical first-30-days algorithm window closed without a momentum strategy.

"

I don't know where my readers gather.
"Anyone who likes good stories" isn't a target audience — it's a hope. Without a reader map, marketing becomes guesswork.

"

Readers love Book 1 but never buy Book 2.
A healthy read-through rate is 50–70%. Below 30%, your back-matter funnel is leaking readers and revenue.

"

I think I'm in the wrong categories.
Amazon has 16,000+ categories; your book can only occupy three. Wrong three, and you're invisible to the readers who'd love it most.

"

I'm spending money but can't tell if it's working.
Without baseline data, every tactic is a shot in the dark. Measurement has to come before spend.

After changes take effect, I review the data again — what moved, what needs refinement — and evolve the strategy accordingly.

About

The difference between 50 copies and 5,000

I've spent over a decade analyzing book markets — studying the patterns that separate visible books from invisible ones. What I've learned: visibility isn't about shouting louder than everyone else. It's about being positioned exactly where your ideal readers are already looking.

I started helping authors apply this understanding informally — friends, writing-group members, people I met at conferences — then more systematically as I refined the analysis process and documented what actually moves the needle. Today I work with authors across 15+ genres who want a clear, honest, data-backed answer to why their book isn't being found.

What I do

  • Base every recommendation on observable data
  • Look at your book's actual market position first
  • Develop genre-specific strategy, not templates
  • Tell you what NOT to do, as often as what to do
  • Measure results and adjust based on real response

What I won't do

  • Fake reviews or rating manipulation
  • Spam tactics or mass outreach
  • Guarantee sales numbers or bestseller status
  • Apply generic templates without analysis
  • Recommend tactics that risk your author career
